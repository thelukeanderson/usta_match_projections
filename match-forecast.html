How this works, and how sure we can be

TennisRecord doesn't publish its formula. Players on the Tennis Warehouse forums matched its numbers against real results and reconstructed the core rule:

  • Match rating = opponent's rating ± a value from your share of games. Winning 6-4 6-4 (60% of games) is worth +0.10, 6-3 6-3 (67%) is +0.20, 6-1 6-1 (86%) is +0.38, and 6-0 6-0 caps out at +0.48. Your own rating doesn't enter into it.
  • Only games count, summed across sets. A match tiebreak counts as one game, so 6-3 0-6 0-1 is 6-10 in games.
  • Doubles: the team's result is compared against the team's combined rating, and any over- or under-performance is split equally between partners, so the gap between partners stays the same. This page compares team averages.
  • The forecast is fitted to real results, not to TennisRecord's table. I tested the table against 1,205 USTA league matches from TennisRecord (Austin and Dallas, 2025–26). It's right for small gaps, but favorites win fewer games than it implies once the gap passes about 0.2. At a 0.4 gap the table says the favorite takes 85% of games, but in practice it's about 70%. So the headline score uses a curve fitted to those matches, and the "to hold your rating" line shows the table's number.
  • Accuracy: the favorite won 74% of the matches. The fitted score is off by about 2.4 games per match on average, and individual matches swing about 3 games either way. Win chances lined up with real results within a few points in every gap range.
  • Dynamic rating: the new match rating is averaged with recent ones (roughly the last three or so). This part is the least certain, so this page doesn't try to predict your next dynamic rating.

Running the table backwards gives TennisRecord's expectation: a gap of 0.20 means the stronger side must win two-thirds of the games (6-3 6-3) to hold its rating. Beyond a gap of about 0.48 the table runs out, and even a 6-0 6-0 win can lower the stronger player's rating.

In the same 1,205 matches, this page's reconstruction reproduced TennisRecord's published match rating within 0.01 for 85% of player-matches and within 0.03 for 90%.

The table is reverse-engineered and occasionally off by about 0.01. Posters also saw bigger mismatches when players were 0.5 or more apart. TennisRecord re-centers everyone at year end, so ratings near January can jump.
